Physical Gold vs Gold ETF vs Sovereign Gold Bonds: Comparison

2026-11-16 · 5 min read

Gold holds a unique place in Indian households, serving both as a cultural symbol of wealth and a financial hedge against inflation. Traditionally, buying gold meant visiting a local jeweler to purchase coins or ornaments. However, modern Indian retail investors can also choose paper and digital forms of the precious metal. When allocating capital to gold, the three most popular avenues are physical gold, Gold Exchange Traded Funds (Gold ETFs), and Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs). Understanding how these three options compare on safety, returns, tax rules, and liquidity can help you pick the right vehicle for your financial goals.

Safety, Storage, and Cost of Acquisition

Physical gold carries significant safe-keeping challenges. Holding jewelry or gold coins at home exposes you to the risk of theft, while storing them in a bank locker adds an ongoing annual rental fee. Additionally, physical gold purchases often involve making charges—which can range from 8% to over 25%—and a 3% Goods and Services Tax (GST).

In contrast, Gold ETFs and SGBs eliminate physical storage risks entirely. Gold ETFs are offered by mutual fund houses regulated by SEBI and are held in electronic form in your Demat account. They track the domestic price of physical gold, charging a small expense ratio (typically 0.3% to 0.8% annually) for managing the fund.

SGBs are government securities denominated in grams of gold. They carry zero storage costs and zero making charges. However, it is important to note that the Government of India has not issued new SGB tranches since early 2024. Today, investors interested in SGBs must check current market availability to buy existing tranches directly from other investors on the stock exchange via a Demat account.

Returns and the Power of Additional Interest

The underlying value of all three options moves in tandem with the domestic price of gold. However, their net returns vary because of operational costs and interest features.

Physical gold returns are often reduced by making charges that cannot be recovered fully upon resale. Gold ETF returns closely mirror domestic spot gold prices, minus the fund's annual expense ratio.

SGBs stand out because they offer an additional return benefit. Apart from tracking the market price of gold, SGBs pay a fixed interest rate of 2.50% per annum on the initial investment amount, paid semi-annually into the investor's bank account.

Consider a simple rupee example: Suppose you buy existing SGBs on the stock exchange equivalent to 10 grams of gold at an initial issue base price of ₹6,000 per gram, totaling an investment of ₹60,000.

  • Annual interest earned = ₹60,000 × 2.5% = ₹1,500 per year.
  • Over an 8-year holding period, total interest received = ₹1,500 × 8 = ₹12,000.

If domestic gold prices rise to ₹9,000 per gram at the end of 8 years, your gold value grows to ₹90,000. Your total proceeds would be ₹90,000 (gold value) + ₹12,000 (interest) = ₹1,02,000. With physical gold or a Gold ETF, you would only capture the price appreciation, without the extra ₹12,000 payout.

Liquidity and Exit Options

Liquidity determines how quickly you can convert your gold investment back into cash.

- Physical Gold: Highly liquid in urgent situations, as local jewelers or pawn shops will readily buy gold coins or ornaments. However, selling physical gold may involve deductions for purity testing or melting losses. - Gold ETFs: Offer high liquidity during stock market hours. You can buy or sell ETF units instantly on the BSE or NSE through your brokerage app at prevailing market prices. - Sovereign Gold Bonds: SGBs have an original tenure of 8 years, with an early redemption window provided by the RBI after the 5th year. While you can sell your SGBs on the stock exchange before maturity, trading volumes can fluctuate. Investors should check secondary market liquidity and order book depth before trading SGBs on the exchange.

Tax Treatment and Rules

Taxation is a crucial factor when choosing between these instruments. Capital gains tax rules in India undergo periodic updates through annual Union Budgets. The tax treatment for physical gold, Gold ETFs, and exchange-traded SGBs depends on whether gains are classified as short-term or long-term based on your holding period.

Additionally, SGBs hold a unique feature under current tax laws: capital gains arising upon full maturity (at the end of 8 years) are completely tax-exempt for individual investors. However, the 2.5% annual interest earned on SGBs is added to your income and taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate. Because tax provisions for debt instruments and commodities evolve, always check the current rules or consult a chartered accountant to evaluate the exact tax impact on your portfolio.

Conclusion

Choosing between physical gold, Gold ETFs, and Sovereign Gold Bonds depends on your primary objective. If you need gold for personal use or weddings, physical gold remains practical despite storage costs. If you want a flexible, highly liquid tool for tactical asset allocation, Gold ETFs are often the most convenient choice. Finally, if you are looking for long-term wealth preservation with passive income, buying existing SGBs on the secondary market offers a compelling blend of gold price tracking and annual interest. Align your choice with your time horizon and liquidity needs to build a resilient financial plan.
