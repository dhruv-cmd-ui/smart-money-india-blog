Physical Gold vs Gold ETF vs Sovereign Gold Bonds: Comparison

2026-11-04 · 4 min read

Gold holds a unique place in Indian households, serving both as a symbol of prosperity during festivals and as a financial hedge against inflation. Traditionally, buying physical gold in the form of jewelry, coins, or bars was the primary choice. However, modern financial markets offer paper and digital gold alternatives that eliminate many traditional hassles. For an Indian retail investor, choosing between physical gold, Gold ETFs (Exchange Traded Funds), and Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) requires balancing liquidity, returns, safety, and tax rules.

Storage, Safety, and Holding Costs

The most obvious difference across these three options lies in how they are stored and the costs associated with holding them.

- Physical Gold: Holding physical gold involves risks of theft or loss. Storing it safely usually requires a bank locker, which incurs an annual fee. Additionally, buying gold jewelry involves paying making charges (often 8% to 25% above the raw gold rate) and 3% Goods and Services Tax (GST). When selling physical gold, jewelers often apply deductions for melting or purity verification. - Gold ETFs: Regulated by SEBI, Gold ETFs are mutual fund units backed by physical gold of 99.5% purity stored in secure vaults. You hold these units digitally in a Demat account, eliminating storage fees, theft risks, and purity concerns. Fund houses charge a nominal annual expense ratio (typically between 0.1% and 0.5%) to manage the fund. - Sovereign Gold Bonds: Issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on behalf of the Government of India, SGBs represent sovereign debt denominated in grams of gold. They can be held in Demat form or as holding certificates. There are no storage charges, no expense ratios, and zero risk of physical theft.

Liquidity and Trading Realities

Liquidity determines how quickly and efficiently you can convert your gold investment back into cash.

- Physical Gold: Highly liquid in an emergency because neighborhood jewelers or pawn shops accept it readily. However, realizing full market value depends on purity testing and finding a buyer who will not apply heavy deductions. - Gold ETFs: Highly liquid during stock market trading hours. You can buy or sell even single units on exchanges like the NSE or BSE at real-time market prices, with funds settling directly into your trading account. - Sovereign Gold Bonds: SGBs have an original maturity period of 8 years. While SGBs are listed on stock exchanges for secondary market trading, liquidity can vary based on buyer demand. Note that the government has not issued new SGB tranches since early 2024. Therefore, investors seeking SGBs today must check current availability on the stock exchange to buy existing series from secondary market sellers.

Returns, Interest, and Tax Treatment

All three options track the spot price of gold, but SGBs provide an added income stream. Physical gold and Gold ETFs generate returns purely through capital appreciation (minus holding costs or fund management fees). In contrast, SGBs pay a fixed interest rate of 2.5% per annum on the initial issue price, credited semi-annually to the investor's bank account.

To see how this interest boosts overall returns, consider a simple numerical example:

Suppose an investor puts ₹1,00,000 into a Gold ETF and another ₹1,00,000 into an SGB issue at the same purchase price. Over a 5-year holding period, assume the market price of gold rises by 50%.

- Gold ETF: The investment grows to approximately ₹1,50,000 (slightly lower after deducting the annual fund expense ratio). - SGB: The underlying gold value similarly rises to ₹1,50,000. Additionally, the investor receives 2.5% annual interest on the initial ₹1,00,000 investment, which equals ₹2,500 per year. Over 5 years, this adds ₹12,500 (₹2,500 × 5) in cash income. The total payout from the SGB becomes ₹1,50,000 + ₹12,500 = ₹1,62,500.

Regarding taxation, the rules for capital gains vary depending on the holding period, whether the instrument is physical or digital, and whether SGBs are sold on the secondary market or held until redemption. Because tax regulations undergo periodic updates, investors should check the current tax rules or consult a professional before executing a trade.

Conclusion

Choosing the right gold investment depends on your primary objective. If you need gold for personal adornment or cultural occasions, physical gold remains the standard choice despite making charges. If your goal is short-to-medium-term asset allocation with maximum trading flexibility, Gold ETFs offer seamless digital convenience. If your goal is long-term wealth creation with an extra income stream, checking the secondary market for existing Sovereign Gold Bonds offers the unique benefit of earning fixed interest alongside potential gold price appreciation.
