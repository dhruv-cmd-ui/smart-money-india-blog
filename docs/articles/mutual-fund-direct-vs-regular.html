Direct vs Regular Mutual Funds: How Much Does It Really Cost You?

2026-11-09 · 4 min read

When you invest in a mutual fund scheme in India, you are given two main options: a Direct plan or a Regular plan. While both plans invest in the exact same portfolio of stocks or bonds under the exact same fund manager, the returns you see in your portfolio over time will not be identical. The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) introduced direct plans in 2013 to allow investors to purchase mutual funds directly from fund houses without paying intermediary commissions. Understanding the structural difference between these two plans is essential for any retail investor looking to build long-term wealth.

Understanding Expense Ratios and Commissions

Every mutual fund scheme charges an annual fee to manage your money, known as the Total Expense Ratio (TER). This fee is deducted daily from the fund’s Net Asset Value (NAV).

In a regular plan, the asset management company (AMC) pays an ongoing trail commission out of the TER to the distributor, bank, or broker who sold you the fund. In a direct plan, there is no intermediary involved, so no commission is paid out. As a result, direct plans have a lower expense ratio than regular plans—typically lower by 0.5% to 1.5% per annum, depending on whether the fund is an equity or debt scheme. While a 1% annual difference might sound minor at first glance, its impact compounds dramatically over long investment horizons.

The Power of 1%: A Concrete Rupee Example

To understand how commissions affect your net wealth, consider the compounding math over a long timeframe. The money deducted as TER does not just leave your account once; you also forfeit all future compounding returns that those deducted rupees would have generated.

Imagine you start a monthly Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) of ₹10,000 in an equity mutual fund for a period of 25 years. Total principal invested over this period equals ₹30,00,000 (30 lakh).

- Regular Plan Scenario: Assuming the fund's underlying portfolio grows at 13% gross, the regular plan delivers a net annual return of 12% after deducting a 1% distributor commission.

  • Monthly SIP: ₹10,000
  • Duration: 25 years (300 months)
  • Net annual return: 12% (1% monthly interest rate)
  • Estimated final corpus ≈ ₹1,89,76,000 (₹1.90 crore)

- Direct Plan Scenario: The direct plan of the same fund has a lower TER, delivering a net annual return of 13%.

  • Monthly SIP: ₹10,000
  • Duration: 25 years (300 months)
  • Net annual return: 13%
  • Estimated final corpus ≈ ₹2,26,72,000 (₹2.27 crore)

- The Real Cost:

  • Difference in final wealth: ₹2,26,72,000 - ₹1,89,76,000 = ₹36,96,000 (approx. ₹37 lakh)

By choosing the regular plan, you end up paying nearly ₹37 lakh in cumulative lost returns and commissions on a ₹30 lakh total investment over 25 years.

When Does Paying a Distributor Make Sense?

Despite the higher cost, regular plans are not inherently bad for everyone. Paying a distributor can be worthwhile if you receive genuine value in return.

- Hand-holding and Discipline: If you lack financial literacy or the emotional discipline to stay invested during market crashes, a competent mutual fund distributor can prevent panic-selling, which often saves far more than 1% of your portfolio value. - Goal Planning: Good distributors help with asset allocation, periodic rebalancing, and choosing funds matched to your personal risk profile. - Execution-only Investors: If you select funds yourself, track your portfolio independently, and handle your own transactions online, paying an ongoing trail commission provides little added benefit. In this case, direct plans are the mathematically superior choice.

How to Switch from Regular to Direct Plans

If you decide to transition existing holdings from a regular plan to a direct plan, keep in mind that SEBI treats a switch as a redemption from one scheme and a fresh purchase into another.

Before making the move: - Check Exit Loads: Ensure the units you are switching have completed their exit load period (typically 1 year for equity funds) to avoid penalty charges. - Calculate Capital Gains Tax: A switch triggers capital gains tax. For equity funds, short-term capital gains tax applies if held under 12 months, while long-term capital gains tax applies above that threshold. Check the current tax rules and exemption limits before placing switch orders. - Process the Switch: You can execute the switch online via the AMC's portal, official registrar platforms, or SEBI-registered direct execution platforms.

The choice between direct and regular mutual funds ultimately comes down to your need for professional guidance versus self-management. If you are comfortable researching funds and managing your portfolio independently, switching to direct plans ensures that every rupee of your hard-earned money remains working for your own financial goals.
