Direct vs Regular Mutual Funds: How Much Does It Really Cost You?

2026-10-30 · 3 min read

When you start investing in mutual funds in India, you are faced with a fundamental choice: should you buy a regular plan or a direct plan? While both options invest in the exact same underlying stocks or bonds and are managed by the same fund manager, they carry very different price tags. Understanding this difference can save you lakhs of rupees over your investing lifetime.

Understanding the Two Routes

When you buy a regular plan, you are investing through an intermediary—such as a mutual fund distributor, independent financial advisor, bank, or online broker. The asset management company pays a commission to this intermediary out of your fund. This commission is permanently built into the scheme's expense ratio, which is the annual fee charged by the fund house to manage your money.

Conversely, a direct plan is purchased straight from the mutual fund house or through platforms that do not accept commissions from AMCs. Because there is no middleman, the expense ratio of a direct plan is lower. Introduced by SEBI to bring transparency, direct plans ensure that you do not pay hidden distribution fees if you choose to manage your investments yourself.

Why a 1% Difference Matters More Than You Think

At first glance, a difference of 0.5% to 1.2% in the expense ratio sounds negligible. If a regular plan charges 1.8% and a direct plan charges 0.8%, saving one percentage point might not seem like a big deal on a monthly bill.

However, mutual fund returns are subject to compounding—and so are the costs. The expense ratio is deducted daily from the fund’s assets before the Net Asset Value is declared. This means you earn returns on a smaller corpus every single day in a regular plan compared to a direct plan. Over years and decades, this seemingly small percentage gap creates a massive divergence in your final wealth.

A Concrete Rupee Example

Let us look at the math with a practical example. Suppose you start a monthly Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) of ₹10,000 in an equity mutual fund for a tenure of 20 years. Assume the fund generates a gross annual return before expenses.

* In the regular plan, after accounting for a higher expense ratio, your net annual return is roughly 10.2%. * In the direct plan, with an expense ratio that is 1% lower, your net annual return is roughly 11.2%.

Using standard compound interest calculations for an investment of ₹10,000 per month over 240 months: At a 10.2% net return, your total corpus after 20 years grows to approximately ₹75 lakhs. At an 11.2% net return, your total corpus after 20 years grows to approximately ₹91 lakhs.

The difference is a staggering ₹16 lakhs. By simply choosing the direct route for the exact same fund, you end up with ₹16 lakhs more in your pocket. You took on the exact same market risk and stayed invested for the same duration, but paid a heavy toll for intermediation in the regular plan.

Making the Switch and Final Thoughts

Choosing direct plans requires you to do your own research, select funds, track your portfolio, and handle periodic rebalancing without professional hand-holding. If you lack the time or confidence to navigate market volatility, paying a qualified human advisor for guidance through a regular plan may be worthwhile. However, if you are comfortable managing your finances independently, direct plans offer a straightforward way to keep more of your hard-earned returns. Fees matter immensely in long-term investing, and keeping your costs low is one of the few variables you can entirely control.
