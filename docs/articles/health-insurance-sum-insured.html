How Much Health Insurance Cover Do You Actually Need?

2026-10-25 · 4 min read

Walking into a hospital today can easily result in a bill that matches the down payment for a home. While most Indian households understand the basic need for health insurance, a vast majority make a critical mistake: they buy a generic sum insured without calculating actual future medical costs. A policy that seemed sufficient five years ago might leave you paying thousands out of pocket today. Determining the right amount of health insurance requires looking past standard recommendations and understanding the realities of modern healthcare expenses in India.

The Reality of Medical Inflation in India

General retail inflation tracks the rising cost of everyday goods, but medical inflation in India moves at a much faster pace. Driven by advanced diagnostic technologies, expensive imported treatments, and rising hospital operational costs, healthcare inflation regularly outpaces general inflation by a significant margin. Treatments for critical illnesses like cancer, cardiac procedures, or organ transplants routinely cost anywhere from ₹10,00,000 to ₹25,00,000 in major private hospitals.

If you bought a base health insurance cover of ₹3,00,000 or ₹5,00,000 a decade ago, that policy is likely severely outdated. Imagine a procedure that costs ₹5,00,000 today. Factoring in a sustained medical inflation rate, that same procedure could easily cost double in ten years. Relying on an old, small policy means you will likely have to dip into your hard-earned retirement savings or investments to cover the shortfall during a medical crisis.

Calculating Your Cover: City Tiers and Family Size

There is no single magic number that works for everyone, but you can calculate a practical baseline by evaluating your city of residence and family structure. Healthcare costs vary drastically depending on where you live.

- Tier 1 Cities: If you live in Mumbai, Delhi-NCR, Bengaluru, or other major metropolises, hospital room rents, nursing charges, and doctor fees are at their highest. For a family of two adults and two children in a Tier 1 city, a base cover of less than ₹10,00,000 to ₹15,00,000 is generally risky. - Tier 2 and Tier 3 Cities: If you reside in smaller towns, treatment costs at local quality hospitals are comparatively lower. However, if a complex case requires shifting to a metro hospital, costs will spike. Even in smaller cities, aiming for at least ₹7,00,000 to ₹10,00,000 is a safer bet.

Consider family size and life stage as well. A young, single professional might manage with a modest independent policy, but a growing family with aging parents requires a robust plan. Since older adults have a higher statistical probability of hospitalization, buying a separate senior citizen policy or ensuring a high family floaters limit is essential. For example, if a family floater covers three members, a single hospitalization event for one member can exhaust the entire limit, leaving the others unprotected for the rest of the year.

Common Underinsurance Traps to Avoid

Buying a high sum insured is only half the battle; you must also read the fine print to ensure the policy actually pays out when needed. Many policyholders fall into traps that silently reduce their payout.

The first trap is the room rent sub-limit. Many budget policies cap the room rent at 1 percent of the sum insured per day. If you have a ₹5,00,000 policy, your room rent limit is restricted to ₹5,000 per day. If the hospital charges ₹8,000 per day for a standard room, you do not just pay the extra ₹3,00,000 out of pocket—proportional deductions apply to doctor fees, medicines, and surgeries as well.

Another trap is ignoring super top-up policies. Buying a standalone ₹25,00,000 policy can be expensive. Instead, you can buy a base policy of ₹5,00,000 and pair it with a super top-up policy of ₹20,00,000 with a deductible of ₹5,00,000. If a hospital bill comes to ₹10,00,000, the base policy pays the first ₹5,00,000, and the super top-up covers the remaining ₹5,00,000. This combination provides a high total cover of ₹25,00,000 at a much lower total premium than a single continuous policy.

Securing your family's financial future starts with ensuring that a health emergency does not derail your long-term wealth goals. Take time to audit your current coverage, account for rising costs, and bridge any gaps before a medical crisis forces your hand.
