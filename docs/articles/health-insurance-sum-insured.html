How Much Health Insurance Cover Do You Actually Need?

2026-11-08 · 3 min read

Most Indian retail investors focus heavily on building wealth through equity mutual funds and stocks, but a single medical emergency can quickly dismantle years of compounding. Health insurance is the primary defensive layer of any sound financial plan. With healthcare costs climbing rapidly across the country, holding an arbitrary cover of ₹3 lakh or ₹5 lakh is no longer adequate for a urban Indian family.

The Reality of Medical Inflation in India

Medical inflation in India routinely outpaces general consumer price inflation, often running at double-digit rates of 10% to 14% annually. Advanced medical technologies, specialized surgeries, and modern drug therapies have improved healthcare outcomes, but they have also escalated hospital bills dramatically. A complex surgery or extended stay in an intensive care unit (ICU) at a private hospital in a major Indian city can easily cost between ₹10 lakh and ₹20 lakh.

When health insurance is insufficient, individuals are forced to break their fixed deposits, liquidate mutual fund Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs), or take high-interest personal loans. Treating health cover as a core asset-protection strategy ensures that long-term investment goals remain uninterrupted during a medical crisis.

Calculating Base Cover by City Tier and Family Size

Determining an adequate sum insured depends primarily on your city of residence and your family structure. Hospitalization costs in metro cities are significantly higher than in Tier-2 or Tier-3 towns due to higher room rates and consultant fees.

For a family floater policy covering two adults and up to two children, a practical baseline framework includes:

- Metro Cities (Mumbai, Delhi-NCR, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata): A minimum base cover of ₹15 lakh to ₹20 lakh. - Tier-1 and Tier-2 Cities (Pune, Ahmedabad, Jaipur, Lucknow, etc.): A minimum base cover of ₹10 lakh to ₹15 lakh. - Tier-3 Cities and Smaller Towns: A minimum base cover of ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh.

To understand why these amounts matter, consider the impact of inflation over time. Suppose a major medical procedure costs ₹6,00,000 in a metro hospital today. Assuming a modest 10% annual medical inflation rate, the formula to estimate the cost of that same treatment 10 years later is:

₹6,00,000 × 1.10^10 ≈ ₹15,56,245

A policyholder who keeps a static ₹5 lakh cover today will face a shortfall of over ₹10 lakh for that same procedure a decade down the line.

Common Underinsurance Traps to Avoid

Even investors who purchase health insurance often fall into specific traps that result in high out-of-pocket expenses during claims:

- Relying Exclusively on Corporate Cover: Employer-provided group health insurance is a valuable benefit, but it usually offers limited coverage (typically ₹3 lakh to ₹5 lakh). More importantly, corporate cover ends immediately if you change jobs, face layoffs, or retire. Buying an individual policy later in life can be significantly more expensive or difficult due to age-related pre-existing conditions. - Ignoring Room Rent Caps: Policies with a room rent limit (such as 1% of the sum insured per day) carry a hidden risk called proportionate deduction. If your policy has a ₹5 lakh cover with a 1% cap (₹5,000 per day), but you choose a room costing ₹8,000 per day, the insurer will proportionately reduce coverage for associated expenses like doctor fees and surgery charges, leaving you with a large unexpected bill. - Buying Low Cover Instead of Using Top-Ups: Increasing a standard base policy from ₹5 lakh to ₹50 lakh directly can carry a steep premium. A more cost-effective approach is combining a ₹5 lakh base policy with a ₹45 lakh super top-up plan featuring a ₹5 lakh deductible. The super top-up kicks in once total annual medical bills cross ₹5 lakh, offering high overall protection at an affordable cost.

Right-sizing your health insurance is not an expense—it is a vital financial buffer. By adjusting your sum insured for medical inflation, accounting for your city's healthcare costs, and avoiding restrictive policy clauses, you ensure that health crises do not derail your long-term wealth creation journey.
