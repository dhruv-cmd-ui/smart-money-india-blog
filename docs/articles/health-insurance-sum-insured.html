How Much Health Insurance Cover Do You Actually Need?

2026-11-20 · 4 min read

A single major hospitalisation can wipe out years of disciplined equity investments. Yet, many Indian retail investors view health insurance merely as a tax-saving instrument under Section 80D, opting for a basic cover of ₹3 lakh or ₹5 lakh. In reality, adequate health insurance is the foundation of financial planning, designed to protect your wealth from the spiralling costs of healthcare. To determine how much coverage you actually need, you must look beyond basic tax benefits and assess your real-world risk exposure.

The Reality of Medical Inflation in India

Healthcare inflation in India consistently outpaces general retail inflation, often hovering between 10% and 14% annually. Advanced diagnostics, modern surgical procedures, and prolonged ICU stays at top private hospitals carry steep price tags that double every few years.

To understand the compounding impact of medical inflation, consider a modern medical treatment that costs ₹6,00,000 today. Assuming a conservative medical inflation rate of 10% per year, the cost of that exact treatment in 10 years will be:

₹6,00,000 × (1.10)^10 ≈ ₹15,56,245

A policy sum insured that feels comfortable today will likely fall short in less than a decade. If your health cover does not keep pace with compounding healthcare costs, your personal savings and investment portfolio will end up funding the deficit.

Calculating Base Cover by City Tier and Family Size

Your required sum insured depends primarily on where you live, your family structure, and the age of the oldest family member. Hospital room charges and procedure costs vary significantly across regions.

- Metro Cities (Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata): A family floater policy (2 adults + children) in a metro city should ideally have a base sum insured of at least ₹10 lakh to ₹15 lakh. For individuals, a minimum cover of ₹7 lakh to ₹10 lakh is recommended. - Tier 1 and Tier 2 Cities: A family floater policy should aim for a base cover of ₹7 lakh to ₹10 lakh, while an individual policy should be at least ₹5 lakh.

For higher protection, combining a base policy with a super top-up plan is the most cost-effective strategy. A super top-up plan kicks in after your hospital bills cross a chosen threshold (deductible). For instance, buying a base cover of ₹10 lakh along with a super top-up of ₹40 lakh (with a ₹10 lakh deductible) provides a total umbrella cover of ₹50 lakh at a fraction of the cost of a standalone ₹50 lakh base policy.

Common Underinsurance Traps to Avoid

Buying a policy is only the first step; ensuring it provides usable coverage during a crisis requires avoiding several common pitfalls:

- Relying Solely on Employer Cover: Corporate health insurance ends the day you leave your job, face layoffs, or retire. Additionally, corporate group covers may have low limits or restrictive terms. Always maintain an independent personal policy. - Ignoring Room Rent Capping: Many policies limit daily room rent to 1% of the sum insured for a normal room and 2% for an ICU stay. If you choose a room above your eligible limit, insurers apply proportional deduction, reducing their payout across the entire hospital bill—including doctor fees and surgery costs. Look for policies with no room rent cap. - Overlooking Sub-limits and Co-payments: Certain policies cap payouts for specific procedures like cataract surgery or knee replacements, or mandate a co-payment clause where you pay a fixed percentage of every bill out-of-pocket. Ensure you understand these terms before renewing or purchasing. - Delaying Policy Upgrades: Buying or increasing insurance cover after developing chronic conditions like diabetes or hypertension becomes harder due to pre-existing disease waiting periods or higher premiums. Upgrade your coverage while you are young and healthy.

Conclusion

Health insurance should never be evaluated as an investment that yields financial returns, but rather as a shield that prevents portfolio depletion. As a practical benchmark, aim for a total health cover—combining a solid base policy with a super top-up—that can comfortably absorb at least two major medical emergencies in a single year without touching your long-term wealth. Review your coverage every three to five years to adjust for inflation and changing family needs.
