Got Your First Salary? Here's a 90-Day Financial Plan

2026-10-30 · 4 min read

Receiving your first salary is a major financial milestone. That notification from your bank brings a fresh sense of independence, but the choices you make during your first three months in the workforce often set the trajectory for your long-term wealth. Instead of letting your earnings slip away on impulse purchases, following a simple 90-day plan allows you to establish strong money habits while still enjoying your hard-earned income.

Month 1: Fix Your Banking and Protect Yourself

The first 30 days are all about laying a clean operational foundation and managing life's major financial risks. Start by completing your banking setup. Ensure your salary account is active, your KYC (Know Your Customer) details are fully updated, and your PAN is linked to your Aadhaar card. Do not forget to add nominees to your bank accounts and employer Provident Fund details.

Next, focus on risk protection before jumping into investments. If your employer provides corporate health insurance, remember that this coverage usually ends if you switch jobs or face employment gaps. Buying a personal health insurance policy early in your career ensures lower premiums and allows you to clear initial waiting periods while you are young and healthy.

If you have family members who depend on your income, you should also purchase a pure term insurance policy. A term plan provides a large life cover at an affordable cost. Aim for a coverage amount equal to at least 10 to 15 times your annual income. Avoid mixing insurance with investment by staying away from traditional endowment plans or unit-linked insurance products, which often carry high fees and offer lower returns compared to dedicated investment options.

Month 2: Create a Financial Cushion

In the second month, your goal is to build an emergency fund. Financial emergencies, such as unexpected medical bills, urgent home repairs, or sudden job loss, can force you into expensive debt if you are unprepared. A solid financial safety net should cover 3 to 6 months of your basic living expenses.

To figure out your target, calculate your necessary monthly expenses, including rent, food, utilities, transport, and existing obligations.

Consider a practical example: Suppose your net monthly salary is ₹40,000, and your essential living expenses total ₹25,000 per month. To build a 3-month safety net, your total target emergency fund is:

₹25,000 × 3 = ₹75,000

If you set aside ₹10,000 from your salary every month into a separate account, you will reach your ₹75,000 target in 7.5 months.

Keep this money easily accessible. Avoid locking emergency funds in high-risk assets or long-term commitments. Instead, park these funds in a high-yield savings account, a sweep-in fixed deposit, or a liquid fund offered by SEBI-regulated mutual fund houses. Liquid funds offer high liquidity along with reasonable safety.

Month 3: Automate Your Investments

By the third month, your risk protections and emergency savings plan are in place, making it time to start growing your wealth. Adopting the principle of "paying yourself first" is the most effective way to build long-term discipline. Instead of investing what is left after spending, spend what is left after investing.

Set up an automated Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) in broad-market equity mutual funds, such as an Nifty 50 index fund. Setting your monthly SIP deduction date 2 to 5 days after your salary date ensures that your money is invested before you have the chance to spend it elsewhere. Index funds offer a low-cost, diversified way to participate in the growth of India’s top companies without requiring you to pick individual stocks.

Before making specific tax-saving investments, check the current rules regarding the New Tax Regime versus the Old Tax Regime. Under the default New Tax Regime, many standard deductions under Section 80C are not applicable. If you opt for the Old Tax Regime, consider tax-saving options like an Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) or the Public Provident Fund (PPF) to reduce your taxable income while investing for the future.

Building financial security is not about timing the market or attempting to get rich quickly. By taking these structured steps over your first 90 days, you turn your monthly paycheck into an organized, long-term wealth creation engine.
