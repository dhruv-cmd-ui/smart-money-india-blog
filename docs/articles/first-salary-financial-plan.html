Got Your First Salary? Here's a 90-Day Financial Plan

2026-11-02 · 4 min read

The notification buzzes: your salary has been credited. That first paycheck brings a genuine sense of freedom, and you should celebrate it with your family or friends. However, the financial choices you make over your first three months will set the trajectory of your wealth for the next decade. Rather than trying to master the stock market on day one, give yourself a structured 90-day window to build your financial foundation step by step.

Month 1: Setting up accounts and mapping cash flows

Your primary focus in the first thirty days is operational hygiene and understanding where your money actually goes.

Start by streamlining your bank accounts. Most employers open a corporate salary account for you. Keep this account for receiving your pay, but consider maintaining a separate secondary savings account for your discretionary spending. Transferring a fixed "fun budget" to this second account prevents you from accidentally burning through the cash needed for your fixed obligations like rent, food, and bills.

Next, establish a simple baseline budget. A reliable starting point is the classic split:

  • 50% for living essentials (rent, food, utilities, commute)
  • 30% for personal lifestyle and wants
  • 20% for future financial goals

For example, let us look at a take-home pay of ₹40,000 per month:

  • Fixed expenses: ₹40,000 × 0.50 = ₹20,000
  • Lifestyle expenses: ₹40,000 × 0.30 = ₹12,000
  • Savings and investments: ₹40,000 × 0.20 = ₹8,000

Before the month ends, verify that your Permanent Account Number (PAN) and Aadhaar are linked, as this is mandatory for accessing financial products in India, and ensure your Know Your Customer (KYC) records are clear.

Month 2: Protecting yourself against sudden shocks

Before putting a single rupee into volatile investments, protect your downside. Young earners often skip insurance because they feel healthy, but unexpected events can wipe out months of hard work.

Begin by purchasing a standalone health insurance policy. Even if your employer provides group health coverage, that cover vanishes the day you switch jobs or face a career break. A basic individual policy of ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh covers hospitalisation costs and prevents a medical crisis from draining your savings.

Next, evaluate the need for term insurance. A term plan provides a fixed payout to your family if you pass away during the policy term. If your parents or siblings depend on your income, buying a term plan in your early twenties is cost-effective because premiums remain locked at low rates based on your entry age. If you have no financial dependents, you can hold off on term insurance for now. Avoid insurance policies marketed as investment or savings plans, as these typically yield low returns while offering inadequate life cover.

Simultaneously, start building your emergency fund. Your eventual goal should be setting aside three to six months of mandatory living expenses. In our ₹40,000 salary example, if essentials cost ₹20,000 monthly, target an emergency reserve of at least ₹60,000 to ₹1,20,000. Keep these funds easily accessible in a high-yield savings account or a liquid mutual fund, completely separated from your daily spending.

Month 3: Automating your long-term investments

Now that your accounts are operational and your risks are covered, you can begin investing. The most sustainable way to build wealth as a beginner is to automate the process through a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP).

An SIP deducts a fixed amount from your bank account every month to purchase units of a mutual fund registered with the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI). This removes the stress of timing market highs and lows.

For beginners, a simple, low-cost broad-market index fund (such as one tracking the Nifty 50) is an ideal core holding. Returning to our ₹8,000 monthly investment allocation:

  • Emergency fund allocation: ₹5,000 per month (until the safety net is full)
  • Index mutual fund SIP: ₹3,000 per month

Schedule your SIP date within three days of your salary credit. When your investments happen automatically on payday, you naturally learn to budget around the money that remains, rather than investing whatever happens to be left over at the end of the month.

The goal of your first ninety days is not to strike it rich quickly, but to eliminate financial chaos. By getting your accounts organized, shielding yourself from emergencies, and setting up automated monthly investments, you create a silent wealth-building engine. Once this foundation is in place, you can spend the rest of your salary without guilt, confident that your future self is already taken care of.
