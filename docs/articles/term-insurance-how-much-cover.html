How Much Term Insurance Cover Do You Need?

2026-10-22 · 4 min read

For most Indian households, buying life insurance is often treated as a tax-saving exercise under Section 80C rather than a tool for financial protection. Many people end up with traditional endowment policies or unit-linked insurance plans (ULIPs) offering modest covers of ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh. If the primary breadwinner passes away, this amount barely lasts a couple of years. A pure term insurance plan provides high life cover at an affordable cost, but deciding the exact sum assured requires more than simply guessing a round figure like ₹1 crore.

The Income-Replacement Method

A reliable way to estimate your required cover is the income-replacement method. This approach calculates the total future earnings your family would lose if you were no longer around to provide for them, adjusted for your working years and major liabilities.

A standard thumb rule is to aim for 15 to 20 times your annual take-home income. However, a more precise formula accounts for three key components:

- Income replacement: (Annual expenses of dependents) × (Years left until retirement) - Liabilities: All outstanding debts, such as home loans, personal loans, and car loans. - Future milestones: Non-negotiable future goals, such as children's higher education and marriage.

From this total, subtract your current liquid financial assets (existing mutual funds, fixed deposits, and provident fund balances). Do not subtract the value of the house you live in, as your family cannot sell it without losing their shelter.

Consider a practical example. Rajesh is 32 years old, earns ₹12,00,000 annually, and plans to retire at 60 (28 working years remaining).

  • His family's annual living expenses: ₹6,00,000. For a conservative baseline using a 15-times multiplier: ₹6,00,000 × 15 = ₹90,00,000.
  • Outstanding home loan: ₹40,00,000.
  • Higher education fund for his child: ₹25,00,000.
  • Total protection needed: ₹90,00,000 + ₹40,00,000 + ₹25,00,000 = ₹1,55,00,000.
  • Less existing savings (PF and mutual funds): ₹25,00,000.

Net term cover required: ₹1,55,00,000 - ₹25,00,000 = ₹1,30,00,000 (₹1.3 crore).

Rounding this up to ₹1.5 crore gives his family a safe cushion against future inflation.

Common Underinsurance Mistakes

Many retail buyers fall short of adequate cover due to a few common traps:

- Relying solely on employer group cover: Corporate health and life covers end the moment you switch jobs, face a layoff, or retire. Buying an individual policy while you are young and healthy locks in lower premiums. - The "₹1 crore trap": Advertisements frequently market ₹1 crore as a universally sufficient figure. While ₹1 crore sounded massive a decade ago, sustained inflation erodes its purchasing power. For a young family with decades of living expenses ahead, ₹1 crore may deplete much faster than anticipated. - Ignoring outstanding loans: Families often face foreclosure on residential property if a borrower passes away without a cover dedicated to clearing the mortgage. - Treating insurance as an investment: Mixing investment with insurance yields sub-par returns and inadequate life cover. Pure term plans ensure every rupee of premium buys maximum protection.

When to Review Your Cover

Your insurance needs are not static; they evolve as your personal balance sheet changes. You should re-evaluate your term cover at specific life stages:

- Marriage: Taking on shared financial responsibilities means another individual depends on your income. - Birth of a child: A child introduces 20 or more years of living and education costs. - Taking a large loan: A significant long-term liability like a 20-year home loan should be immediately matched with additional cover. - Substantial salary hikes: As your lifestyle and household expenses rise, your cover multiplier should reflect the higher standard of living.

Many Indian life insurers offer life-stage increment options, allowing you to increase your sum assured by 25% to 50% during events like marriage or childbirth without undergoing fresh medical tests. If your base policy lacks this feature, you can simply purchase an additional term policy to bridge the gap.

Getting your term insurance cover right is the foundation of a sound financial plan. By stripping away investment clutter, calculating your family's actual expenses and liabilities, and regularly updating your cover, you ensure that your dependents remain financially secure through life's uncertainties.
