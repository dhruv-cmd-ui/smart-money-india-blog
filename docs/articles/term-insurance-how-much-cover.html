How Much Term Insurance Cover Do You Need?

2026-11-05 · 3 min read

When it comes to financial planning for your family, buying term insurance is arguably the most crucial step. Yet, a vast majority of Indian retail investors make a common mistake: they pick a random, round-figure sum insured—like ₹1 crore just because it sounds large—without doing the actual math. If you pass away prematurely, will that amount actually sustain your family's lifestyle, pay off your liabilities, and fund your children's milestones? To answer this, you need a scientific approach to calculating your coverage rather than relying on guesswork.

The Income-Replacement Method: The Gold Standard

The most practical and reliable way to calculate your required term insurance cover is the income-replacement method. Instead of focusing on your current age or what you can afford in premiums, this method focuses on what your family needs to maintain their standard of living in your absence.

To calculate this, you need to look at four core components:

  • Annual household expenses that need to be replaced.
  • Outstanding liabilities (such as home loans, car loans, or personal loans).
  • Future milestone goals (such as your children's higher education and marriage).
  • Existing financial assets and investments (such as mutual funds, fixed deposits, or EPF) that can be subtracted from your total need.

Let us look at a concrete rupee example. Imagine Rajesh, a 30-year-old salaried professional living in a Tier-1 city.

  • His current annual household expenses are ₹8,00,000.
  • He has an outstanding home loan of ₹30,00,000.
  • He estimates he needs ₹25,00,000 for his child’s future education.
  • His total existing investments and savings add up to ₹15,00,000.

Using the income-replacement method, Rajesh wants to ensure his family can generate an income equal to his current expenses, adjusted for inflation over a long time horizon. A common rule of thumb is to multiply your annual expenses by 15 to 20 times, depending on your age and expected retirement timeline, to ensure the payout can generate enough passive income (say, through safe instruments) to replace your salary.

Let us do the simple math for Rajesh:

  • Expense replacement corpus: ₹8,00,000 × 15 = ₹1,20,00,000
  • Add outstanding liabilities: ₹30,00,000
  • Add future goals: ₹25,00,000
  • Subtotal requirement: ₹1,20,00,000 + ₹30,00,000 + ₹25,00,000 = ₹1,75,00,000
  • Subtract existing assets: ₹175,00,000 - ₹15,00,000 = ₹1,60,00,000

Rajesh’s ideal term insurance cover is ₹1.6 crore.

Common Underinsurance Mistakes to Avoid

Many Indian investors fall into traps that leave their families vulnerable. One major error is buying a term plan early in life—say, at age 25—and never updating it, even after marriage, children, and a massive home loan upgrade. Another frequent mistake is confusing endowment or ULIP policies with term insurance. Traditional insurance mixes investment with insurance, offering low returns and meager cover. A ₹10-lakh endowment policy is useless for income replacement; you need a pure term plan that offers high cover for a fraction of the cost.

Additionally, people often forget to account for inflation. Your household expenses today will double or triple in 15 years. Failing to factor in rising education and healthcare costs means your family's payout will lose its purchasing power much sooner than expected.

When to Review Your Term Cover

Life is dynamic, and your insurance needs change as you cross different milestones. You should ideally review your term insurance cover every few years or whenever a major life event occurs. Key trigger points for a cover review include getting married, welcoming a child, taking on a large long-term liability like a home loan, or experiencing a significant permanent jump in your baseline lifestyle expenses. If you find your current cover falls short during these reviews, you can either buy an additional term policy or check the current rules of your existing insurer for top-up options.

Securing the right amount of term insurance is the ultimate act of financial care for your dependents. By moving away from arbitrary numbers and using the income-replacement method, you can buy peace of mind knowing your family's future is genuinely protected against life's uncertainties.
